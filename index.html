How we estimate these prices
  • Starting point: national averages from a 2026 analysis of 10,000 VIN-based windshield quotes by Glass.com ($337 without a driver-assist camera, $507 with one, $259 for recalibration), plus published price ranges for other glass.
  • Your vehicle: we adjust for body style, brand, model year, and whether it has a driver-assist camera.
  • Glass type: aftermarket glass is the default because it is what most shops quote. Genuine (OEM) windshield glass is priced about 50% higher on a plain windshield and about 80% higher on one with a camera bracket, based on a national chain's OEM upcharges and our own OEM jobs.
  • Recalibration: checked against rates published by Seattle-area glass shops ($250 to $450 depending on the method) and AAA's $360 national figure.
  • Your area: in the greater Puget Sound area the price level is set from local evidence: a national chain's online quotes for Seattle, the Puget Sound Consumers' Checkbook survey of area shops, and our own invoices. Elsewhere we adjust national figures using 2024 regional price data from the U.S. Bureau of Economic Analysis.
  • The four ranges: "Fair" runs from the typical price to about 25% above it. "Good" is up to about 15% below typical, "Great" is lower than that, and "Likely overpaying" starts more than about 25% above typical. Cutoffs are rounded to the nearest $5. Compare them with the pre-tax price on your quote.
  • Reality check: we compared these estimates with 6,057 paid cash invoices from our Washington shops (January to November 2026). The typical estimate was within 15% of the actual price on 71% of jobs and within 25% on 85%, and our own prices ran a median 10% below it. We also compared it with a national chain's online cash quotes for seven vehicles in Seattle. That chain's cheapest service tier averaged 6% below our typical figure before its fees and add-ons, ranging from 16% below to 7% above. On door and back glass, the same chain's quotes for four vehicles ran 11% to 76% above our typical figure, and our typical figure sits inside the $150 to $400 range the Puget Sound Consumers' Checkbook reports for most side and rear windows.
  • What we can't see: options such as a heads-up display, heated glass, or acoustic glass, which only a VIN lookup confirms. That is why this is a range and not a quote.

Estimates are out-of-pocket prices before tax. They come from national data adjusted for your vehicle and area, not from a survey of shops in your zip code.
